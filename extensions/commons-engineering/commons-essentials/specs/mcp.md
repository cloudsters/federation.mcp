# Commons MCP

*One service shape at every level: who asks is one user in one shell, the shell's grant fixes what may be read, the answer stays within it or becomes a proposal, and every result says what it rests on.*

## 1 · Goal

A commons is used by people, agents and systems that do not work in its repository, and they need its model in their own context. Its MCP serves its graph (`graph`) and its CORE to them, each within a grant the commons decides, and it is the one door through which they propose work. It has no truth of its own: it reads its sources at a cut, writes only items, and keeps only the operational state it states. This specification decides two things for its reader: **whether a result of a commons' MCP can be relied on**, meaning that it was produced for this caller's grant alone and says what it rests on and, for a write, what became of it; and **what such a service may hold and do**.

## 2 · Boundary

**Belongs here:** the users of a commons by shell and their grants (`access.yml`), DOCK's entries by pattern, and what the set-up generates from them, the platform's teams and the ruleset of each repository of a commons; the connection to `federation.mcp`; the serving pipeline and its three tools; the envelope; proposals and their receipts; reading upstream; operational state and use records; budgets; local and hosted operation.

**Does not belong here:**

| Matter | Where it belongs |
|---|---|
| what the graph is, how an answer is computed, what an answer must say of its sources, non-disclosure claims | `graph`, `graph-engine` |
| what is released, its parts, digests and states, and how a commons learns of a newer release | `library`; a commons reads a library's index itself |
| readings from a system of record | `fabric`, a specification of its own; a reading enters only as a source of the graph (`graph` R3) |
| items, workshops and what a proposal becomes there | `flow`, `flow-workshops` |
| the shells themselves | `model` |
| what a shell's team may do in the CORE's repository beyond the ruleset, and how a change becomes a pull request | `core`, `flow` |
| which repositories a commons has, and which workshop a shell works in | `flow-workshops` |
| an identity provider, tokens and keys as a transport | the protocol's authorisation, outside the CORE |
| an operator's memberships, prices and service terms | the operating commons |

**Touches** `specification`, `core`, `core-knowledge`, `model`, `graph`, `graph-engine`, `library`, `extension`, `flow` and `flow-workshops`. **Rests on** Holger's words: the MCP that helps a commons understand and use its own CORE, delivered with it, the federation's on that basis later (commons.o#93, comment 6424755304); the loops at every level, Fabric taking signals in and the MCP serving the result inside (commons.o#94, comment 6436299151); the shells and S6, a visibility class naming the outermost shell that may see a thing (commons.o#80, 24 October 2026); use capture redefined in MCP (commons.o#68, comment 6468387523); MCP started (commons.o#94, comment 6472436861); M1, nothing served without sign-in (6473067076); the KISS pass, three tools (commons.o#98); the shells as groups of users, a technical user naming its accountable human, the platform's teams from the same file, and the MUST connection at ROAM, free and with no named user (commons.o#100); his decisions on it: OPEN reads by default, only as users the commons placed there (A1), DOCK an interface admitting a partner's staff by pattern (A2), the MUST naming `federation.mcp` and the Commons for the discipline (A4), SEED and CORE seeing the whole repository (A5), and the ruleset and the checks on a pull request generated in the set-up like the teams (A7) (commons.o#100, comment 6495768947); the Commons as what the Federation holds in common (A4), the shell pairs, a repository per pair (A6), and no exception from the pull request for SEED (H2 struck) (6496414850). It answers the EA's findings (commons.o#94, comments 6439368849 and 6473229155) and keeps the bridge's evidence boundaries (6468579999).

## 3 · Terms

All terms resolve in the glossary. *Caller*, *grant*, *receipt* and *use record* keep their ordinary meaning, made precise by part 5. A *caller* is a user, an account an entry of DOCK admits, or one that signed in as no one; a *grant* is a shell's, as one user or entry holds it. *The Commons* is what the Federation holds in common, Commons Engineering, the discipline, being part of it; the Federation is the interest body of all its stakeholders.

## 5 · Structure

The schema is `schemas/mcp.schema.json`. Its root is `.commons/access.yml`; its `$defs` hold `user`, `envelope`, `receipt` and `use`.

**The pipeline** (R2): **1** the account the sign-in reports → its one user in `access.yml` and that user's grant, else the entry of DOCK its verified address matches, else the `public` grant; **2** the grant → the sources the caller may see, every other source withheld before anything is read; **3** the tool within that cut, or one item opened; **4** the envelope with its provenance and, for a write, a receipt, and one use record.

**`access.yml`**, in `.commons/` beside `commons.yml`, which the commons writes:

| Field | Holds |
|---|---|
| `classes` | each visibility class of the register mapped to the outermost shell that may see it, CORE or further out; `public` is always ROAM's, and `member: OPEN` unless the commons says otherwise (S6); personal data is a class like any other |
| `public` | ROAM's grant, for a caller that is no user: `sources` and `budget`, no writes; `none` when absent |
| `federation` | the address of `federation.mcp` (R24) |
| `shells` | each shell's grant, which its users share: `sources`, `writes` (`commons`, `model`, `maker`; none when absent), `budget`; a shell not listed reads its cut and writes nothing |
| `users` | each user once, in one shell: `account` (`account:` for a platform account, `key:` for a key, as the sign-in reports it, never a secret), `shell`, and optionally `sources`, `writes` and `budget` narrowing the shell's, and `expires`; a technical user, which every key is, names `accountable`, the account of a human user, and `expires`; an entry of DOCK may hold `match` (`*@<domain>`) in place of `account`, with `accountable`, the human user in CORE or further in who holds the contract on the commons' side, and `expires`, the contract's end (R27); every `expires` is a calendar date, its day the last on which the entry holds |
| `budget` | the default `depth`, `nodes` per result and `calls` per hour and user |
| `keep` | the days a use record is kept |

The shells are MODEL's, from the centre: SEED, CORE, PEER, OPEN, DOCK, ROAM. A user without `accountable` is a human's; at ROAM a caller need not be a user.

**The set-up** (R26): `serve_mcp.py <core> --setup` prints what it generates. `teams`: one per shell from SEED to OPEN, each with the platform accounts of the shell's users that have not expired and grant something. `repositories`, one ruleset per pair: `SEED|CORE`, the CORE's repository, with `merge: [SEED]`, `push: [SEED, CORE]` and `checks: [check_core]`; `PEER|OPEN`, which holds the work with the model and never the model, with `merge: [SEED, CORE, PEER]`, `push: [SEED, CORE, PEER, OPEN]` and `checks: [check_flow]` until that repository has a check of its own; both with `branch: main`, `change: pull request`, `bypass: none` and `merged: delete branch`. The platform applies a pair's ruleset where the commons has that repository (`flow-workshops`); how a pull request names its item and how its branch is named is `flow`'s.

**The tools** (R2):

| Tool | Takes | Answers from |
|---|---|---|
| `answer` | `question` | GRAPH's answer path (`graph` R12–R14), any question of the commons' model, knowledge, changes, decisions and exchange |
| `node` | `id` | the node, its content as its source holds it at the cut, the record, claim, rule or pattern itself, and its visible stated and computed edges, so that a reader without the repository uses what `answer` found |
| `propose` | `to` (`commons`, `model` or `maker`), `text`, `anchor`, `scope`; for `maker` also `release`, `record`, `kind` (`correction`, `question`, `proposal`) and `source`; `request` | opens one item (R12, R13) |

What a commons gave and got (`answer`) is read from its own record: its `release` events, the receipts of its proposals kept on the items they came from, the versions it installed and the records it adopted; for a release, what came of it returns only as the released aggregate of explicit answers, kept apart from no response.

**The envelope** (`$defs/envelope`): `grant` (the user's shell, or `public`), `offer` and `gets` (R28: per shell, and for this caller, `reads`, `sources` where narrowed, `writes`, `budget`), and either `refused` with its reason, naming nothing outside the caller's cut, or the result, `answer` (a GRAPH answer, `graph` `$defs/answer`), `node` or `receipt`, with its `provenance`: `cut`, `runs`, `method` (`stated-only`, `lexical`, `model`, `lookup` or `write`) and `upstream`, and for a write what the server `resolved`.

**A receipt** (`$defs/receipt`): `request` (the request id), `item`, `to`, `state` (`sent`, `created`, `taken up`, `accepted`, `refused`) and `at`.

**A use record** (`$defs/use`): `at`, `tool`, `grant` (the shell, or `public`), `outcome` (`served`, `refused`, `budget`), `cut` (a digest over the cut's entries).

**Operational state** (R20): a session lives in the service's memory for the session or the token's lifetime; a cache in the service, keyed by grant, policy and cut, until any of them changes or is withdrawn; a user's calls of the period in the service, the user as a digest, for the period; request ids and receipts with the commons that operates the MCP, the user as a digest, until the item is closed and `keep` has passed; use records with that commons for `keep` days.

## 6 · Checks

`tools/check_mcp.py <core>` reads a CORE's `access.yml` against its graph's register and runs the reference pipeline `tools/serve_mcp.py` as every user and as a caller that is no user. `tools/serve_mcp.py <core> --as <account> <tool> [args]` is the reference path: the four steps for `answer`, `node` and `propose`, over GRAPH's reader (`answer_graph.py`), with a local outbox standing in for the platform's board. It reports **fail**, **should** or **gap**, and exits with 1 only on a fail.

| Check | Tests |
|---|---|
| `access` | `access.yml` validates and names `federation.mcp`; every class of the register is mapped to a shell, none to SEED alone; each account, and each domain, stands once; no user is wider than its shell in sources, writes or budget; a technical user names a human user accountable for it and an expiry, and stands no further in nor holds more than that human; an entry by pattern stands in DOCK, matches one domain and names a human in CORE or further in and an expiry; an expired user grants nothing (R3, R4, R24, R27) |
| `pipeline` | every tool runs the four steps, an account an entry of DOCK admits gets that entry's grant, and a caller that is no user gets the `public` grant or nothing (R2, R3) |
| `projection` | asked as each user, `answer` and `node` show no node, edge, cut entry or finding of a withheld source and no computed edge resting on one, and say that something was withheld; a withheld node and a missing one get the same answer (R6) |
| `envelope` | every result validates against `$defs/envelope`, carries the offer of every shell and what its caller gets (R28), carries its provenance where it was served, its answer validates against `graph`'s `$defs/answer`, and none names a user (R7) |
| `budget` | an answer and a node hold no more nodes than their budget, depth 0 shows no edge, and a call beyond the calls of the period is refused, naming no withheld node (R8) |
| `write` | a proposal sent twice by one user returns one receipt; two users of one shell under one request id get an item each; a request id reused for another operation, a payload field the server builds and a destination the user's grant does not list are refused; a receipt starts `sent` (R12, R13) |
| `state` | every call leaves one use record, which validates and holds no user or question (R20, R21) |
| `setup` | the teams generated from `access.yml` hold each platform account that grants something in its shell's team and in no other, and no key or entry by pattern; there is a ruleset per pair, by which only the inner shell's team or one further in merges into `main`, by a pull request whose checks have passed and with no exception, the outer shell's team pushing branches; SEED's team is not empty (R26) |
| `review` | a reading by the human accountable, or by an agent that has only this file, of R1, R18, R19, R22, R24 and R25 |

**Not built yet** (`specification` R28): the board: receipts stay `sent`, since the reference has no board whose events could move them, and creating items atomically under crash and concurrency is untested; the user and its verified address come from the transport, which the reference only receives; the generated teams and ruleset are printed, not applied to the platform; the connection to `federation.mcp` is named, not tested; reading upstream (served parts, another level's MCP); the transport and sign-in by the protocol's authorisation; the qualification of `keep` and cache invalidation, which no fixture tests and no result reports as passed; the qualification as a real session through a transport, of which the reference runs the local half: two users in two shells, a restricted source, an edge derived from it, the installed extract as the upstream source, a withdrawn technical user, a stale source and a retried proposal. The live `mcp.commons.engineering` is the bridge of commons.o#94 (6468425261): its correspondence to the captured source is unverified, and its daily probe shows availability only.

## 7 · Example

**Minimal:** a commons that serves its users and nothing public, one of whom is accountable for an agent working on its own with a narrow key and holds the commons' contract with a partner, whose staff reach it through DOCK until the contract ends:

```yaml
classes: {public: ROAM, member: OPEN, personal: CORE}
public: none
federation: "https://federation.mcp.example"
budget: {depth: 2, nodes: 5, calls: 60}
shells:
  SEED: {writes: [model, commons, maker]}
  OPEN: {writes: [commons, model, maker]}
users:
  - {account: "account:ada", shell: SEED}
  - {account: "account:ben", shell: OPEN}
  - {account: "account:cleo", shell: OPEN, writes: [commons]}
  - {account: "key:repair-agent", shell: OPEN, accountable: "account:ada", sources: [registry], writes: [], expires: "2026-12-31"}
  - {match: "*@partner.example", shell: DOCK, accountable: "account:ada", expires: "2026-12-31"}
keep: 90
```

The set-up it generates: the teams SEED `ada` and OPEN `ben`, `cleo`, the key and the partner's entry in none; and a ruleset per pair, by which only `ada` merges into the CORE's `main`, by pull request like everyone else.

**Complete:** [the tool library served through its MCP](tools/tests/mcp/run_tests.py): GRAPH's example with its `access.yml`, asked as a SEED user, as OPEN users, through technical users and as a partner's account through DOCK, with a restricted source, an edge derived from it, a withdrawn key, a stale source, and every defect the EA demonstrated in the first draft as a probe that fails closed; `check_mcp.py` reads it with 0 fail.
